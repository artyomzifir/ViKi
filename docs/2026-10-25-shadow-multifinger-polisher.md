# Shadow multifinger grasp and placement pilot (2026-10-25)

This is an opt-in diagnostic on the frozen 190-frame HO-Cap G10_2 Shadow Hand episode. The original plan and the earlier hand-only and palm-only negative results remain unchanged. The hypothesis was that the palm-corrected pinch loses the object because only the thumb and index finger carry sustained load. We changed the *saved finger position commands*, including acquisition before the first recorded contact, while holding the previously selected −6 mm object-frame palm/arm trajectory and all 190 timestamps fixed. The result is a substantially better three-digit carry in this one scene, but neither the grasp-stability diagnostic nor the original released-placement task gate passes. This is **not** an accepted production plan or a success-rate result.

The independent pre-search [digit probe](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_digit_probe.json) found palm-only carry force above 0.05 N on thumb/index/middle/ring/little for 89.8%/83.7%/6.4%/3.3%/0% of physics steps, and thumb with at least two other digits together for only 7.8%. At frame 60 the middle, ring and little distal gaps were about 1.4, 6.3 and 13.2 mm; the [geometry probe](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_finger_gap_probe.json) informed three directed seeds. This was a contact-geometry hypothesis, not a request to maximize raw mesh contact points. Positive normal force was summed per named digit at each MuJoCo physics step; the multifinger event requires the thumb and two *different* other digits each above 0.05 N in the same step.

## Frozen experiment

The first pool had 64 commands: the zero residual, bounded signed axis probes, three gap-informed flexion seeds, and fixed-seed eight-dimensional Latin-hypercube points. The parameters were independent MF/RF/LF flexion synergies (J1/J2/J3 weights 1/.7/.35), MF/RF/LF J4 abduction, LF J5 cupping and TH J2 opposition. Their bounds, all 64 vectors and the fixed seed are in [phase1_protocol.json](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/phase1_protocol.json). The smooth residual rises over frames 10–30, is held through frame 125 and fades by 151. The arm and timestamps are bitwise unchanged from the palm-only input; commands after frame 151 are unchanged. Zero residual copies the input float32 command matrix bit for bit. Nothing is hard-clipped to make a candidate appear feasible.

Each candidate passed URDF joint-position and **saved segment-velocity** checks, then Pinocchio floor and screened self-collision checks with that candidate's *new passive finger trajectory*. The latter uses the nearest eight retained URDF self pairs per frame; adjacent and nominal-contact pairs are excluded by the existing kinematic model. MuJoCo alone evaluates collision with the table and other tracked objects. There is no exhaustive geometric proof of scene clearance at the command-construction stage.

For every feasible command we replayed to frame 60, froze all 28 controls for two seconds, and measured object pose relative to the wrist. The static diagnostic requires >20 mm lift over the post-settle initial height, no positive-force target support from table/other objects and no robot-table contact throughout the hold. It additionally requires peak slip <10 mm, peak fall <10 mm, last-second peak relative drift <5 mm, peak relative rotation <10°, and thumb plus at least two other digits above 0.05 N in at least 80% of the final second. Ranking used a declared continuous score of capped peak slip/fall/drift/rotation plus a triad-contact penalty and a 1000-point penalty for not being airborne and unsupported. Only the best eight airborne candidates could proceed to full 190-frame replay; the original and palm-only commands were controls. The final full-replay rank was the original frozen score plus `100 × (1 − carry thumb+two fraction)`, with the unchanged target gate taking precedence. Static success never substitutes for the target gate.

The first pool yielded **17/64 feasible**: 23 failed command limits, 24 failed screened self-clearance and none failed the floor screen. Only two feasible candidates remained airborne without target support for the full two-second hold; none met its stability thresholds. The best full-run command, MF flexion +0.12 rad, engaged thumb/index/middle through 97.6% of carry physics steps. Its source-specific [phase1 report](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/phase1_report.json), [feasibility ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/phase1_feasibility.jsonl), [static ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/phase1_static_ledger.jsonl), and [full ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/phase1_full_ledger.jsonl) retain all acceptance and rejection reasons.

After observing that result, we explicitly declared **one** local exploratory follow-up of 32 commands, for 96 total evaluations. It used MF flexion {0.08, 0.10, 0.12, 0.14} × RF flexion {0, 0.04, 0.08} × LF flexion {0, 0.08} rad (24), plus MF {0.10, 0.12} × THJ2 {−0.04, −0.02, 0.02, 0.04} rad (8). All other coordinates were zero. The [follow-up protocol](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_protocol.json) records this data-driven chronology before the run; it did not change the physical model, gates, score or timing. All 32 passed the same command/geometry screen, 23 stayed airborne without support, and **0/32 passed static stability**. The best eight airborne commands received full replay. There was no further search expansion.

The follow-up result has thumb/index/middle force above 0.05 N in every carry step; ring and little still contribute zero sustained force despite the small RF command. Its two-second hold is genuinely lifted and unsupported with three active digits throughout the final second, but slips 13.4 mm, falls 17.2 mm, drifts 9.1 mm in the last second and rotates 23.6°. It therefore fails the predeclared static gate. An independent [frozen-control hold probe](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_final_hold_probe.json) also found force lost within two seconds when frozen at carry frames 90 and 98, so the contact is not a stable static grasp at later poses. Its [full report](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_report.json), [feasibility ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_feasibility.jsonl), [static ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_static_ledger.jsonl), and [full ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_full_ledger.jsonl) preserve the raw results. The final time-stamped [diagnostic command export](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_final_commands.npz) includes joint names and source plan/track/URDF/mesh and palm-input hashes; [trajectory.npz](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_trajectory.npz) retains each physics-step digit force, per-frame digit fractions, target motion, support, wrist-relative position and original evaluator diagnostics.

The 51.33 mm carry p95 is dominated by the *pickup* interval: reference-position p95 is 59.47 mm for frames 42–59, 18.84 mm for frames 60–98 and 23.99 mm for frames 99–125. Thus the coordinated fingers prevent the original mid-carry loss, but do not fix delayed pickup. Reference positions and rotations come from the tracked HO-Cap object trajectory, not an independent ground-truth success label. The object is later disturbed during placement: reference error p95 rises to 101.69 mm over retreat frames 151–189. The selected command has final error 100.58 mm, and although the object is released and table-supported over the terminal interval, it misses the unchanged <30 mm carry/final criteria and the terminal speed criterion. The full-scene gate also fails: a neighboring object drifts about 17.3 mm in this fixture, which an independent stationary-robot control already showed can occur without the edited grasp. We did not relax the 5 mm scene threshold. Negative contact separation reported by the imported mesh contact model reaches about 10 mm, a limitation on physical fidelity.

A separate bounded [palm-fade ablation](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_palm_fade_results.json) kept the −6 mm palm correction later but left the original fingers; carry contact and loss were virtually unchanged. A separate placement control used the *first-pool MF +0.12 command* as input, not the follow-up winner. Three explicit new finger-opening motions failed the existing self-clearance screen before rollout. Three source-finger-preserving arm stop/retreat commands passed; the best anchor at frame 130 reduced that input's final error from 92.67 to 70.40 mm and met terminal release/support/settling, yet still failed the target gate. Its final orientation differs from the recorded object by 94.37° and its center is at z=0.007 m versus reference z=0.075 m: the object is toppled, not upright placed. These controls isolate a placement-motion problem without claiming that a combined new grasp and placement solution succeeded. They were not tested on the four-point sensitivity grid. Their [protocol](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_placement_protocol.json), [ledger](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_placement_ledger.json), [report](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_placement_report.json), and [orientation evidence](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_placement_orientation.json) are durable; working output lives under `data/experiments/hocap-rl/shadow_placement_source_release_20261025/`.

The small held-out physical sensitivity grid varied object mass {0.06, 0.10} kg and robot/object friction {0.9, 1.2}, without using those values in nominal search. The selected follow-up command failed the target gate in **all four** cases, despite 94.5–100% three-digit carry force fraction. Its final reference error ranged 8.27–175.99 mm, so nominal contact continuity does not imply robust placement. See [followup_sensitivity.json](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/followup_sensitivity.json). These four variations of one scene are sensitivity checks, not a dataset generalization or success rate.

## Reproduction and scope

The runner uses the same unassisted `make_scene` and original strict `rollout` from `experiments/hocap_shadow_polisher.py`: four free official meshes, 0.08 kg nominal mass, robot/object friction 1.2, table friction 1.0, 0.0005 s physics step, unchanged URDF effort limits and position gains, and no weld, teleport, object-pose forcing, collision-filter or friction edits. It uses the existing `shadow_palm_polisher_20261025/final_commands.npz` as the exact −6 mm palm input. Raw working results are under `data/experiments/hocap-rl/shadow_multifinger_polisher_20261025_v2/` and `shadow_multifinger_followup_20261025/`; compact copies live in [the durable results directory](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/). A partial first launch was stopped **before static ranking** to strengthen the airborne/peak-drift validity gate; it is marked `ABORTED.json` and was not mixed with the fresh `v2` run.

On this configured machine, from the repository root:

```bash
docker compose -f docker-compose.experiments.yml run --rm sim experiments/hocap_shadow_multifinger_polisher.py --output /app/rl-output/shadow_multifinger_polisher_20261025_v2
docker compose -f docker-compose.experiments.yml run --rm sim experiments/hocap_shadow_multifinger_followup.py --output /app/rl-output/shadow_multifinger_followup_20261025
docker compose -f docker-compose.experiments.yml run --rm sim experiments/hocap_shadow_multifinger_polisher.py --replay /app/rl-output/shadow_multifinger_followup_20261025/final_commands.npz --output /app/rl-output/shadow_multifinger_independent_replay_20261025
```

Use new output directory names or move aside previous runs before rerunning; the runners fail closed on existing ledgers. Replay verifies source hashes, ordered joint names, float32 command shape, exact original timestamps, bounded residual reconstruction, joint limits and saved-file metrics. The selected named command matrix was also fed to the untouched `hocap_shadow_replay.py` through its original plan-input fields. [Independent comparison](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_multifinger_reproduction.json) found bitwise-identical object position/orientation, actual joints, and contacts, with identical compiled masses, friction, gains and collision filters. The separate placement control passed the same cross-runner comparison; see the [parent review](../paper/results/shadow_multifinger_polisher_2026_10_25/parent_review.json). The source recordings, meshes and URDF are local ignored data and required for reproduction.

The selected command had 0.0136 rad/s minimum saved command-velocity margin and 0.0239 rad/s sampled actual-joint velocity margin; those sampled values do not prove continuous-time hardware safety. Its minimum command-position margin is only about 9×10⁻⁸ rad because the frozen source plan lies very close to a URDF endpoint. No production or hardware safety claim follows.

The full Docker test suite passed **543 tests, 11 skipped**; the simulator experiment suite passed **86 tests**. The two search runs took 292.1 s and 461.0 s wall time respectively on this host. The new code is experiment-only: [multifinger controls](../experiments/shadow_multifinger_controls.py), [first runner](../experiments/hocap_shadow_multifinger_polisher.py), [local follow-up](../experiments/hocap_shadow_multifinger_followup.py), and [regression tests](../tests/unit_tests/experiments/test_shadow_multifinger_controls.py). No default retarget or replay behavior changed.
