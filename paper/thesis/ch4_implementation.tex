\chapter{Implementation and Evaluation}

\section{Software and Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:arch}

The Python implementation separates capture, calibration, perception,
preparation, retargeting and export. FastAPI serves offline jobs and the
browser inspection interface; MuJoCo experiments use a separate image. Each
stage writes a durable episode artifact. The \texttt{rec.npz} hand extraction,
\texttt{cln.npz} prepared motion, \texttt{object\_models.npz} scene model and
\texttt{plan.h5} robot plan are not interchangeable evidence. Physical-robot
replay remains a stub and is not used here.

\label{sec:tasks}
One demonstrator moved a blue $40$~mm cube from a red support cube to a
green target cube on a $1200\times700$~mm table. The nominal red--green
separation is $400$~mm. Two hardware-synchronized Azure Kinects, each on an
independent USB host controller, recorded the demonstrations. The robot is a
simulated old-generation UR10 with a Robotiq 2F-85 two-finger gripper; no
physical UR10 was present. The analysis cohort is the 78 nonempty episodes
listed in \texttt{data/experiments/2026-10-09-full-audit.json}; it is not
all directories or failed capture attempts. The source episodes share one
approximately fixed cube arrangement. Local-cohort numbers were computed
from the then-saved artifacts by \texttt{scripts/build\_thesis\_results.py}.
Those local source artifacts were subsequently lost; the numerical record
must be regenerated from a new cohort before a reproducible submission.

\section{Capture and Hand Reconstruction}
\label{sec:current-results}

The 78 episodes contain 11,688 paired frame groups with device timestamps
for both cameras. The absolute device-timestamp gap has a median of
$155\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, a 95th percentile of $167\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ and a maximum
of $178\,\mu\mathrm{s}$; none exceeds $1$~ms. This is a pair-timing diagnostic,
not a bound on RGB--depth exposure differences or 3-D position error. The
host-arrival \texttt{sync\_bounded} flag measures another clock and must not
be substituted for the device gap. The large $400\times500$~mm ChArUco
calibration made cloud overlap visually usable after an earlier small-board
problem, but no independent measurement certifies millimetre accuracy.

The multi-view hand stage solved 232,368 of 244,671 joint slots (95.0\%)
across the 78 episodes. Episode-wise coverage has a median of 97.6\% and
10th--90th percentiles of 88.9--100.0\%. Seventy-three episodes passed the
articulated Prepare gate; five retain only partial \texttt{cln.npz}
checkpoints and are \emph{not} valid prepared demonstrations. In the 73
completed artifacts, 10,463 of 10,993 frames (95.2\%) carry supported
geometry. The longest unsupported run per episode has median 3 frames,
95th percentile 21.2 and maximum 53. These are coverage measurements, not
absolute hand-pose errors for the local Kinect rig: there is no independent
motion-capture reference. The separate HO-Cap experiment below measures a
different capture system against its published annotations.

\section{Object Models, Retargeting and Physics Replay}
\label{sec:object-replay}

The original object-quality gate accepted 44 of the 73 prepared episodes.
Independent scratch rebuilds with two-view track recovery raised acceptance
to 70 of 73, recovering 26 previously rejected episodes without changing
canonical scene files. Three remained rejected. On these 70 accepted models,
the median manipulated-object surfel extent along its three object axes was
$46.2\times47.7\times49.7$~mm; 35 of 70 had at least one axis above 50~mm,
although the nominal cube edge is 40~mm. A partially visible surfel shell is
not a measured solid-cube bounding box, but the discrepancy warns against
treating the model as exact grasp or collision geometry. Cube yaw is also
symmetry-ambiguous without a marked face.

Forty canonical \texttt{plan.h5} files exist: 38 older-mode and two later v3
plans. Their estimated-target position RMSE has median 6.2~mm, p90 7.7~mm
and maximum 122.0~mm. The 122.0~mm plan is an explicit quality failure,
not evidence of usable IK. The 26 newly recovered object models were
retargeted in a separate scratch branch: 22 passed its geometric plan gate,
two failed retargeting and two failed plan quality. Canonical and scratch
counts have different modes and gates and are not added into one yield rate.

MuJoCo replayed all 24 produced scratch plans with free cube bodies and a
50~Hz held-command controller. Under the provisional contact model,
threefold-slowed commands and a simulator-only 0.10~rad grasp-pressure
command, 18 of 24 passed the released, stable-stack, grasp-integrity and
contact-physics checks. Among the 22 geometrically accepted plans, 17 also
passed replay; one geometrically rejected plan happened to stack in this
simulator but remains rejected for large target error. Robot contact with
the red support occurred in all 24 replays and green-support contact in two.
Thus ``physics-successful'' means stable stacking under these \emph{specific}
assumptions, not collision-free, physically safe or real-robot-valid motion.
Static old-UR10 wrist forward kinematics agreed between Pinocchio and
MuJoCo to $4.9\times10^{-16}$~m on 15 saved poses, and the gripper pad-gap
models agreed within $2.6\times10^{-9}$~m on 15 commands. These are checks
between software representations of the same robot, not hardware accuracy.

\section{Local Layout Transfer and Learning}
\label{sec:res-policy}

One successful recovered human-motion plan,
\texttt{2026-10-06\_11-01-57}, seeded local object-relative variations. Red
and green cube XY positions were shifted independently; the blue cube began
on red. Full-path IK and the same MuJoCo replay gate accepted 46 of 48
sampled layouts at $\pm20$~mm per axis, and 27 of 48 at $\pm50$~mm. These
are \emph{candidate generation yields} in two different random sweeps, not
source-diverse human demonstrations or uniform reachability guarantees.
Every accepted sweep still touched the red support. The 73 successful
teacher plans from these sweeps were split by layout (51 train, 8 validation,
14 held-out test), but all derive from the \emph{same} human episode and
simulator.

A first, plain 53-dimensional state behavioural-cloning policy was trained
on 17 separate physics-successful source replays, with an episode-level
12/2/3 train/validation/test split. It achieved \emph{zero} released stacks
in 17 closed-loop evaluations and did not lift the blue cube. Its low
offline action-prediction error did not translate into task success.
The later layout-conditioned model is deliberately narrower: it observes
normalized time and the \emph{initial}, simulator-provided red/green XY
offsets, and predicts six UR10 joint-command corrections to the single
source trajectory. Gripper commands, timing, contact model and source
reference are fixed. It does not observe images or react to subsequent
object motion, and it is supervised imitation, not reinforcement learning.

On 14 held-out \emph{teacher-success-selected} layouts, the learned
correction passed 14/14 replays versus 4/14 for the unchanged source
commands on the same scenes. With the checkpoint frozen, a second seed
sampled 30 new $\pm50$~mm layouts \emph{without} teacher-success filtering:
the learned controller passed 30/30 versus 0/30 for unchanged commands.
The direct path-IK teacher had accepted only 18 of these 30; its 12
rejections are solver outcomes, not proofs of physical unreachability.
Wilson 95\% intervals for the learned success fractions are [0.785, 1.000]
and [0.886, 1.000], respectively. These intervals condition on the fixed
policy, simulator and layout sampling scheme; they do not quantify
between-training-seed, source-motion or dynamics variation. All 14 and all
30 learned replays still contacted red with the robot.

\section{Placement Quality and Contact Limitations}
\label{sec:quality}

A separate \emph{in-sample} search compared four successful variants on
each of 18 teacher-accepted layouts from the independent seed. Ranking
combined simulated support-contact impulse, XY centre error and
orientation errors while keeping released stack success as a hard gate.
Selecting the lowest-penalty candidate per layout reduced mean blue--green
centre error from 9.39 to 4.09~mm and cube-symmetry-aware relative
orientation error from 17.56 to 14.38 degrees. All 18 selected variants
still stacked, but mean red-contact impulse stayed at 1.222~N\,s and red
contact occurred in every scene. This is optimization \emph{on those same
18 layouts}, not a held-out performance estimate; the learned policy was
not retrained on selected variants.

\section{Statistical Interpretation and Remaining Measurements}
\label{sec:statistics}

Episodes, layout trials, paired frames and joint slots have different units
and denominators. Frame-level counts are not independent camera-calibration
replicates. All policy comparisons above use matched layouts and the same
low-level controller, but there is one selected motion source, one trained
checkpoint and one provisional physics model. No data-free trained RL policy,
visual policy, source-disjoint test, real UR10 evaluation or independently
measured hand/object ground truth is available. Consequently this chapter
establishes a constrained simulation proof of concept for one task and a
strong failure diagnostic for support-object clearance; it does \emph{not}
establish general-purpose learning, collision-free demonstrations or
Real-to-Sim-to-Real transfer.
