\chapter{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{ch:disc}

\section{What the Results Establish}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The 78-episode audit demonstrates an operational camera-to-plan route with
visible attrition. Five recordings fail the articulated hand gate. Of 73
prepared episodes, the original object gate accepts 44 and an independent
track-recovery scratch procedure accepts 70. The 22/26 geometric retarget
result applies only to the previously rejected and then recovered subset;
the 40 canonical plan files belong to a different branch. Adding these
counts into one apparently homogeneous end-to-end percentage would
misrepresent their selection and different planner modes.

Dynamic execution is no longer merely prospective. Seventeen of 22
geometrically accepted recovered plans produce released stable stacks in
the tested MuJoCo model. Yet the robot hits the red support in every one of
the 24 replayed scratch scenes. Consequently the simulation result is
evidence for a task predicate under provisional contacts, not for a safe
collision-free trajectory oracle. The 122~mm canonical target-RMSE outlier
also shows why solver convergence alone is not a plan-quality gate.

The failed plain BC experiment matters: 17 physics-successful source
rollouts and low offline action loss did not yield a single closed-loop
stack. The later 14/14 and 30/30 outcomes are for a \emph{different},
phase- and initial-layout-conditioned policy that retains a successful
source command trajectory. It cannot be described as the same BC method
with a larger dataset. The one source plan itself stacked in 22/22
near-fixed layouts, exposing how little variation those original scenes
provided. The learned correction has a measurable advantage over unchanged
commands on shifted layouts, not over a newly designed geometric controller
or a separately trained no-ViKi model.

\section{Error Sources and Reference Frames}
\label{sec:errbudget}

The stage metrics use different references and cannot be added as an
absolute geometric error budget. The separate HO-Cap pilot obtained
10.50--11.67~mm hand-joint MPJPE on two imported sequences, but its camera
pair was selected on those same frames and its semi-automatic MANO labels do
not measure local Kinect calibration, object orientation or robot execution:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \emph{Camera geometry.} The large ChArUco board restored visually
        useful cloud overlap, but neither a green automatic validation
        report nor cloud nearest-neighbour agreement proves absolute
        calibration accuracy. Board scale, coverage, depth calibration and
        near-field finger returns remain potential biases.
  \item \emph{Temporal pairing.} The 155~$\mu$s median Kinect
        device-timestamp gap is small for this cohort, but exposure timing,
        RGB--depth offsets and rolling object motion may still cause
        moving-hand disagreement. Host-arrival drift is a separate metric.
  \item \emph{Perception.} Solved-joint and supported-frame fractions
        measure coverage, not position accuracy. Object identity and track
        recovery decide which frames enter the downstream sample. The
        $46.2\times47.7\times49.7$~mm median surfel extents for a nominal
        40~mm cube are a concrete model-geometry warning, though surfel
        bounds are not true solid dimensions.
  \item \emph{Retargeting.} Position RMSE compares a robot plan to estimated
        targets generated within the same pipeline. It does not validate
        the human trajectory or the true cube pose. The red support is not
        enforced as an obstacle in the current IK.
  \item \emph{Physical replay.} Pad compliance, friction, cube mass,
        command slowdown and the simulator-only grasp-pressure bias affect
        pickup and release. The exceptionally small wrist-FK and gripper-gap
        discrepancies only verify agreement between software models.
\end{enumerate}

Errors need not be independent. Correlated hand and cube offsets can partly
cancel in an object-relative grasp, while an oversized model can bias both
grasp side and obstacle clearance. An external calibration target at table
and carry heights, and a marked asymmetric object for orientation, would
provide independent checks that the current data cannot supply.

\section{Attribution and Statistical Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

The 14-layout held-out comparison is paired, but the layouts were sampled
only from teacher-successful cases. The 30-layout independent-seed test
instead includes all samples in its specified $\pm50$~mm range, including
12 that the direct path-IK generator rejected. This is a useful robustness
check on the \emph{frozen} learned checkpoint, not evidence that the rejected
layouts are physically unreachable. Wilson intervals describe uncertainty
from finite layout trials under the chosen sampling distribution; they do
not include training-seed, source-demonstration or physics-model variation.

Every one of the 73 teacher layouts shares the same human motion and
simulator. Layout-disjoint splitting prevents literal leakage of a target
layout into training but cannot test source-motion generalization. The
time-conditioned model knows the task phase and source reference. It reads
simulator-truth cube positions once at the start, so it does not solve
visual recognition or closed-loop recovery after disturbances. A fixed
template is a meaningful no-learning comparator, but it is not a trained
no-demonstration baseline. No causal claim about the benefit of ViKi
relative to RL from scratch is supported.

The 18-layout placement-quality search is explicitly in-sample:
variants are selected and assessed on the same scenes. The mean centre
error improvement is real within those saved replays, but it is not a
generalization estimate. Its unchanged 1.222~N\,s mean red-contact
impulse and universal red contact show that better placement alone does
not fix pickup clearance. The success predicate must be presented with
these diagnostics, not as a collision-free success rate.

\section{Scope and Implications}
\label{sec:implications}

For the narrow cube task, passive RGB-D capture can lead to simulated
stable-stack examples and a learned local correction that outperforms
unchanged source commands on new placements. This is the completed
proof-of-concept claim. It neither establishes a generally useful
trajectory oracle nor a policy deployable on a UR10. Simulation contacts
and executed states have not been validated against hardware; no
Real-to-Sim-to-Real transfer is claimed.

The most valuable additional recordings would be \emph{measurement-only}
controls rather than more repetitions to train the current model:
held-out large-board poses across the task height, static known geometry
at both camera ranges, a marked cube or asymmetric object for orientation,
and disjoint human trials to expose source dependence. These controls
could quantify errors without motion capture, but they cannot turn
self-consistency into millimetre ground truth by assertion. Pouring is
outside the study scope.
